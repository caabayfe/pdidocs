Predict Configuration Item model integration

Consider the following when deciding the retraining frequency:
  • The velocity of data and business conditions that generate the frequency.
  • How often you want the feedback to come into the model creation process.
  • How often the selected input fields get newer data patterns in the Incident Management application.

To effectively triage incidents, plan to make changes to your Incident Management application based on how the prediction output is consumed, for example, auto-populated or shown as a recommendation.

A scripted extension point and a business rule is implemented when a model is integrated into your existing business process. The business rule controls when predictions are triggered. By default, the order of the Predictive Intelligence Workbench business rule is configured to run predictions first. Refer to product documentation regarding business rules, for more information.

Once your model is integrated and you are satisfied with predictions, consider initiating a roll-out and monitoring strategy in your production environment. Engage a few IT service desk agents at a couple of different locations, if possible, to participate in the roll-out program. Other recommendations to support a successful full-scale deployment include, creating FAQs for users and assembling a stabilization team to monitor misrouted incidents.

Glossary

Net automation:
The percentage of incidents managed by machine learning that are correctly predicted.
Precision:
The percentage likelihood that a machine learning prediction is accurate.
Coverage:
The percentage of your business that machine learning will impact.

What to do next

After successfully integrating the model into your existing application, and when you are comfortable with the new process, click the completed use cases. Export them, and any associated artifacts, by creating the update set and importing it into production.

Maintaining good data hygiene is of paramount importance for machine learning to work effectively. Machine learning projects trigger new data governance initiatives within business organizations.

In successful implementations, good change management practices are often instituted. For example, a FAQ page for agents giving clear instructions on how they can contribute to improve machine learning models.
