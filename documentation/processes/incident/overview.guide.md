## What an incident is

An **incident** is anything that stops you working or makes your work harder than it should be:
a laptop that won't start, an application that shows an error, a printer that's out, a network
that's slow. If you are asking for something new (access, software, equipment), that is a
**request**, not an incident. Use the service catalog for it.

Each incident gets a number starting with **INC**. Quote it whenever you talk to the service desk.

## Words you will see

| Word | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Caller** | The person the incident is for. The caller is told about progress and confirms the fix. |
| **Priority** | How quickly the incident must be fixed, from **1 - Critical** to **4 - Low**. It follows from the impact and the urgency; nobody types it in. |
| **Assignment group** | The support team responsible for the incident. |
| **Assigned to** | The person in that team working on it. |
| **Additional comments** | Messages the caller can see. Writing one emails the caller. |
| **Work notes** | Internal notes for support teams. The caller never sees them. |
| **On Hold** | The incident is waiting for something: you, a planned change, a problem fix or a vendor. |
| **SLA** | The deadline for a first response and for the fix, set by the priority. |

## Deadlines at a glance

Deadlines run on the support hours below. A deadline stops counting while the incident waits for
you (*On Hold - Awaiting Caller*) and while it is *Resolved* waiting for your confirmation.

| Priority | First response within | Fixed within | Support hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 - Critical | 15 minutes | 1 hour | around the clock |
| 2 - High | 1 hour | 8 hours | weekdays 8:00 to 17:00 |
| 3 - Moderate | 4 hours | 1 working day | weekdays 8:00 to 17:00 |
| 4 - Low | 8 hours | 2 working days | weekdays 8:00 to 17:00 |
