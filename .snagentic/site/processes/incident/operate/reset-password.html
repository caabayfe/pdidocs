    14. Reset a caller's password

    For: Service desk agentWhere: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror 9f3658cfb43d)

    When to use it NEW

    A caller who is locked out, or has forgotten their password, can ask the service desk to reset it. From the incident you are working on, Reset Password opens a guided tab: pick the user, check who they are, then reset the password or unlock the account. E01 E03

    On pdi2 it can't be used yet: the only service-desk process is inactive, so the tab has no process to offer. E14 E15 shared/Q07 The steps below are what an agent will see once it is switched on; none of the screens were opened on 2026-10-18, so they follow the guided tour and the pages' labels. E06 Q81 Until then, the self-service process lets callers reset their own ServiceNow password E16, and the Password Reset form in Employee Center logs an incident for other applications (see Report an incident).

    Who can do it NEW

    • Agents with the password_reset_service_desk role. E01 E21
    • Only on an incident assigned to you: the button and the tab check it. E01 E13

    The same button is on an interaction assigned to you. E22

    Reset a password NEW

    Transcript (6 steps)
    1. Start. On an incident assigned to you, choose reset password in the action bar. You need the service desk password reset role.
    2. Identify. Select the user and the password reset process, then continue to verify. On pdi2, no service desk process is active yet, so this is where it stops today.
    3. Verify. Ask the caller for their email address. The tab shows what the answer should be. Continue if it matches, or reject.
    4. Reset. Choose reset password, which also unlocks the account, or unlock account if that is all they need.
    5. Tell them. Read the generated password to the caller, using the phonetic reading, and confirm you have. It is also emailed to them, and they must change it at their next login.
    6. Failures. After three failed verifications, the caller is blocked from password reset for a day. Don't keep trying.

    Download the video (MP4)

    1. Open the incident in the Service Operations Workspace and choose Reset Password in the action bar (on a narrow screen, under More options). E01 E04 A Reset password tab opens. E02
    2. Identify: in Select user, choose the caller; in Select password reset process, choose the process; then Continue to verify. E04 E07
    3. Verify: the tab tells you what to ask, "Ask the user to provide their" email address, and shows what the answer should be. If it matches, choose Continue; if not, Reject. E05 E08 E09 E17 Q38
    4. Reset: the tab shows whether the account is locked. E05 Choose:
      • Reset password, which also unlocks the account; or
      • Unlock account, when the caller only needs to get back in. E10
    5. The new password is generated for you and shown under New Password, with a Phonetic reading to spell it out. Read it to the caller, tick I have read the password to the user, and Close. E11 E14

    Don't close the tab half-way: "The session won't be saved if you close the tab." E12

    What the caller receives NEW

    • The password, read out by you. E11
    • An email to their address, "Your new password", that contains it. E14 E18 Q39
    • At the next login, the platform asks them to choose their own password. E14

    If verification fails NEW

    After 3 failed attempts the caller is blocked from password reset for 24 hours. E19 E20 Don't keep trying: put the incident on hold and ask the caller to come back with the right details, or escalate to the platform team.

    Tips

    • Never write the new password in work notes, comments or chat. The caller already gets it by email. E18
    • Reset only for the person who asks, or check with your lead first: the caller of the incident is not always the person locked out.
    • A locked account with a process that can't unlock it ends on an error; ask the platform team. E23
