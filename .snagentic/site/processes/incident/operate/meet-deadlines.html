    15. Meet an incident's deadlines

    For: Service desk agent, Resolver, Team leadWhere: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror d13485ddb833)

    Every incident carries deadlines, called SLAs: one for the first response and one for the fix. The priority decides how long each one is (see Priority and deadlines). This page is about what you do with them while you work.

    The two clocks NEW

    ClockStartsPausesStops
    Responsethe incident has a priority and an assignment group E01never E01it has left New and has an assignee E01
    Resolutionthe incident has a priority E02On Hold - Awaiting Caller, and Resolved E02Closed E02

    Assigning a person to a New incident puts it In Progress at once E04, so Assign to me meets the response SLA in one click.

    The resolution clock completes only at Closed, but it is paused from the moment you resolve. So in practice it is met if you resolve in time and the caller doesn't reopen. Waiting for a change, a problem or a vendor does not pause it. E02

    When the priority changes NEW

    The priority SLAs count from when the incident was created, not from when the priority was set. When you raise the priority, the old SLAs are cancelled and the new ones start, counted from the creation time, with past pauses taken off. E03 Raising a two-hour-old incident to 1 - Critical therefore gives it a 15-minute response SLA that is already breached. That is intended: the clock reflects how long the caller has waited.

    Group and supplier deadlines NEW

    Two support groups have their own resolution deadline (an OLA) while an incident is assigned to them, and one storage device has a supplier deadline (an underpinning contract). They run beside the priority SLAs and stop when the incident leaves the group or the device. E05 E06 You see them in the Task SLAs list, not in the timers below.

    Where you see them NEW

    Transcript (7 steps)
    1. Start. The resolution SLA attaches as soon as the incident has a priority, and it counts from when the incident was created.
    2. Pause. The clock pauses while you wait for the caller, and from the moment you resolve the incident. Waiting for a change, a problem or a vendor does not pause it.
    3. Half way. At half the time, the assignee gets a warning email, and so does the person who supports the configuration item.
    4. 75%. At three quarters, the assignee and their manager are warned.
    5. Breach. At the deadline, both get a breach email. The clock keeps running until the incident closes.
    6. Close. The SLA completes when the incident closes. Because it pauses at resolved, resolving in time meets it, unless the caller reopens.
    7. Watch it. In the workspace, open record information in the side panel. Under SLAs and timings you see the response and resolution timers, and view all SLAs lists the others.

    Download the video (MP4)

    1. On a saved incident, open Record Information (the heartbeat icon) in the side panel. E07 E08
    2. Under SLAs and timings there are two timers: Response SLA (Time to respond) and Resolution SLA (Time to resolve). E09 E10 Each shows the priority SLA that applies, including one already met. E12 E13
    3. A timer reads "… remaining" or "…% time elapsed", Breached, Out of Schedule outside its working hours, or No matching SLA when none applies yet. E14
    4. View all SLAs lists every SLA on the incident, OLAs included, as Task SLAs. E11

    The same list is the first one under Related records › Task SLAs. E15 On the classic form, Show SLA Timeline draws the SLA's history. E23

    The warning emails NEW

    The SLA engine emails as each deadline approaches:

    WhenResponse SLAResolution SLA, OLA, contract
    50% of the time usedthe assignee E16the assignee, and the person who supports the incident's CI E17 E21
    75%the assignee E16the assignee and their manager E17
    breachedthe assignee and their manager E16the assignee and their manager E17

    The subject says "SLA … associated with INC… has reached 75%" or "… has breached". E18 E19 Your manager's copy also says who the incident is assigned to. E20 An SLA that is already breached when it starts, after a priority change, sends its breach email straight away. E22

    Tips

    • Assign a person as soon as you take an incident: the response SLA wants an assignee, not just a group.
    • Ask the caller with On Hold - Awaiting Caller, not In Progress and a comment: only the first pauses the clock.
    • Resolve when the fix is in; don't wait for the caller's confirmation.
    • A breach email is no reason to rush the fix wrongly; tell the caller what is happening instead.
