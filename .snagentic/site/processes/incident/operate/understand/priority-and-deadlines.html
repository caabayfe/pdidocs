    Priority and deadlines

    Checked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror fcd5bcad66af)

    How urgent is it?

    Every incident gets a priority, from 1 - Critical to 4 - Low. Nobody types it in: it follows from two answers. E01 E02

    • Impact: how many people, or how much of the business, is affected.
    • Urgency: how soon it hurts. High means work has stopped; Low means it can wait.
    Impact ↓ Urgency →HighMediumLow
    High1 - Critical2 - High3 - Moderate
    Medium2 - High3 - Moderate4 - Low
    Low3 - Moderate4 - Low4 - Low

    What you choose

    You set both Impact and Urgency; the Priority field fills in and is read-only. A caller on the portal sets only the urgency, so check the impact when you pick the incident up. An email marked High importance sets both to High, so it arrives as 1 - Critical: check that it really is. E13 When you change the priority of an existing incident, a work note saying why is required. E12

    Transcript (4 steps)
    1. Two questions. Two questions decide the priority. Impact, how many people are affected, and urgency, how soon it hurts. The priority follows by itself.
    2. Critical. High impact and high urgency make it critical. That means a first response within fifteen minutes, and a fix within an hour, around the clock.
    3. Moderate. Mixed answers give a moderate priority: a response within four working hours, and a fix within a working day.
    4. Low. Low on both, or low and medium, gives a low priority, to be fixed within two working days.

    The deadlines

    The priority sets two deadlines: a first response (someone has taken the incident) and a fix.

    PriorityFirst response withinFixed withinHours that count
    1 - Critical15 minutes E031 hour E07around the clock
    2 - High1 hour E048 hours E08weekdays 8:00 to 17:00
    3 - Moderate4 hours E0524 working hours, about 3 working days E09weekdays 8:00 to 17:00
    4 - Low8 hours E0648 working hours, about 5 to 6 working days E10weekdays 8:00 to 17:00

    The fix deadline stops counting while the service desk is waiting for the person who reported it, and while the incident is resolved waiting for confirmation. E07

    What this means for you

    The incident's deadlines are in its Task SLAs list. Working hours are counted in the caller's time zone. E09 E10 Waiting for a change, a problem or a vendor does not stop the fix deadline; only waiting for the caller, and being resolved, do. E07 The full list of SLA definitions, including group OLAs, is on Working an incident - configuration.
