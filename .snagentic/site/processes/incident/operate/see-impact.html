    13. See what the incident's configuration item affects

    For: Service desk agent, ResolverWhere: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror d13485ddb833)

    When to use it NEW

    An incident usually names one Configuration item (the server, application or device that fails) and one Service. Before you set the impact, or when calls keep coming in, look at what else depends on that item: other services, other users, a whole site. The workspace gives you three ways:

    You want toUse
    see the item's upstream and downstream connectionsthe dependency view button beside the field E01
    keep a list of what the incident touchesAffected CIs E07
    keep a list of the services hitImpacted Services/CIs E07

    What you find feeds the Impact you choose (see Set the priority). The CMDB side of the same views is in See what depends on a configuration item.

    Open the dependency view NEW

    Transcript (6 steps)
    1. Start. Start by filling the configuration item on the incident, or its service or service offering.
    2. Open. Choose the tree icon beside the field. It appears only when the field has a value, and any agent can use it.
    3. Map. A map tab opens in the workspace, centred on the item, with five levels of connections around it.
    4. Affected. The incident's own item is kept in the affected CIs list for you. Add any others you find.
    5. Impacted. Refresh impacted services rebuilds the list of services hit, from the incident's item. Services you added by hand stay.
    6. Decide. Use what you found to set the impact, or to propose a major incident when many services are hit.

    Download the video (MP4)

    1. Open the incident in the Service Operations Workspace and fill Configuration item (or Service, or Service offering).
    2. Choose the tree icon beside the field: CI dependency view, Service dependency view or Service offering dependency view. It appears only when the field has a value, and needs no special role. E01 E02 E03
    3. A Map tab opens in the workspace, centred on that item and showing five levels of connections around it. E04 E05
    4. Use Apply filters to narrow the CIs by field, and Open in new map to centre the map on another CI. Related items and Attributes show more about a selected CI. E06

    The map can also create and delete connections between CIs, and warns that deleting one "might result in an invalid CMDB state". E06 Leave the CMDB's relationships to its owners: if a connection looks wrong, tell the CI's support group instead of fixing it from the incident.

    Keep the affected and impacted lists NEW

    The incident keeps two lists, both on the record's related lists and in the Impact section of the incident overview. E07 E08 E09

    • Affected CIs: what the incident touches. The incident's own CI is added for you, and replaced when you change the CI. E10
    • Impacted Services/CIs: the services hit. The incident's Service is added for you in the same way. E11
    • Assets, in the overview: the affected CIs that have an asset record. E09

    To add more, in the overview's Impact section, choose Add on the list and pick configuration items; filter by Configuration Class to find them faster. E12 E13 E14 Remove takes rows away, except the incident's own CI: change or clear the field on the form instead. E15 Both buttons appear only while the incident is active. E12 E14

    On the related lists of the main form, Add and Remove on these two lists appear only if the platform team has switched list editing on for incidents. E20 The mirror can't show whether it is (see the open questions).

    Refresh the impacted services

    Refresh impacted services on the Impacted Services/CIs list (for agents with the itil or sn_incident_write role) rebuilds the list from the services that the incident's CI affects. E16 E18 It runs in the background; you get "Refresh impacted services for INC… has completed". E17 Services you added by hand stay. E18 On pdi2 it starts from the incident's CI only, not from every affected CI. E19

    Outages and locations NEW

    • Outages: record that a CI was down, with Create outage on the Outages list. E07 E23
    • View impacted locations, in the overview, lists the locations hit. E08 E24

    Who is told NEW

    People can subscribe to a configuration item. When the state of an incident with affected CIs changes, they get "Your Subscribed CI … is being affected by INC…". E21 E22 So keeping Affected CIs right also tells the CI's subscribers.

    Tips

    • Set the CI before you open the view: the button isn't there while the field is empty. E01
    • An affected CI with many dependants is a reason to raise the impact, or to propose a major incident (see Escalate an incident).
    • Prefer Refresh impacted services to adding services one by one, then add by hand only what the CMDB doesn't know.
