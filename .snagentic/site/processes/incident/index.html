    Incident management

    Log, prioritise, route, work and resolve incidents in the Service Operations Workspace, against deadlines set by priority, and keep the caller informed until each one is closed.

    Where: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror 9f3658cfb43d)

    Who this is for

    • Service desk agent — logs, sorts, prioritises and routes incidents, often while on the phone with the caller
    • Resolver — a member of a support team who investigates and fixes the incident
    • Team lead — a support lead with extra rights, such as closing incidents on a caller's behalf
    • Major incident manager — takes charge when one problem hits many people or a critical service

    What you can do

    • 1. Log and categorise an incident
      Record the caller's issue on the incident form so the right team can act on it.
    • 2. Set the priority
      Choose impact and urgency; the priority, and with it the deadline, follows.
    • 3. Assign an incident
      Get the incident to the right team and the right person.
    • 4. Work on an incident
      Investigate, keep the caller informed, put it on hold when you wait, and watch the deadlines.
    • 5. Resolve an incident
      Record the fix so the caller is told and can confirm it.
    • 6. Close, reopen or cancel an incident
      Finish an incident once the fix is confirmed, reopen it when it is not, or cancel one logged by mistake.
    • 7. Escalate and link related work
      Raise a major incident, or create a change, a request or a child incident from an incident.
    • 8. Communicate about an incident
      Send scheduled email updates about an incident to the people it affects, and see its communication plans.
    • 9. Discuss an incident with colleagues
      Start a Sidebar discussion from the incident, invite colleagues, a group's on-call people or the caller, and find it again later.
    • 10. Create and link knowledge from an incident
      Attach an article that helped, write a new one from a resolved incident, or report that one is missing.
    • 11. Find answers with Agent Assist
      Search knowledge, the catalog, other incidents and outages from the side panel, and attach, link or copy what you find.
    • 12. Fill in and reply with templates
      Fill in an incident with a record template, reply with a response template, and write emails with Compose Email.
    • 13. See what the incident's configuration item affects
      Open the CI's dependency map, and keep the incident's affected CIs and impacted services up to date.
    • 14. Reset a caller's password
      Check who the caller is, then reset their password or unlock their account from the incident assigned to you.
    • 15. Meet an incident's deadlines
      Watch the response and resolution SLAs in the workspace, know what starts, pauses and stops them, and what the warning emails mean.

    Lifecycle

    1. Newlogged; nobody is working on it yet
    2. In Progresssomeone is assigned and working on it
    3. On Holdwaiting for the caller, a change, a problem fix or a vendor
    4. Resolvedthe fix is in; the caller can confirm or reopen it
    5. Closedfinished; it can no longer be reopened
    6. Cancelednot needed after all (for example a duplicate logged by mistake)
