    4. Work feedback and knowledge gaps

    For: Author, Knowledge managerWhere: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror fcd5bcad66af)

    When a feedback task reaches you

    A reader who flags an article creates a knowledge feedback task for it, with their comment as its description. E01 E02 On pdi2 that is the only feedback that creates work: a No to Helpful? or a low star rating is counted but creates no task. E03 Q02

    The task goes to the article's author, or to its reviser when someone revised it. E04 Q19 You get "Feedback Task … has been assigned to you". E17

    Gaps reported from incidents are feedback tasks too, without an article and without an assignee: the knowledge managers pick them up. E04 E15 Q05 See Create and link knowledge from an incident.

    TypeComes from
    Internal feedbacka reader's flag E01
    User feedbacka rating or No with a reason (not asked for on pdi2) E01 E03
    Knowledge gapa gap reported from an incident E05

    E07 Q37

    In the Service Operations Workspace, the Knowledge lists have Your feedback tasks and, separately, Your tasks - flagged. E08

    Work a feedback task

    Transcript (8 steps)
    1. Read. Find an article by searching Employee Center or browsing its knowledge page, and open it.
    2. Votes. Helpful, the star rating and comments are counted on the article. On pdi2 they ask for no reason, so nobody is asked to act on them.
    3. Flag. To have something fixed, open actions and choose flag article. Write what is wrong, and submit.
    4. Task. The flag becomes a knowledge feedback task for the article's author, or its last reviser. They are emailed.
    5. Fix. The assignee works on it, asks the reader for more if needed, and edits the article or creates a new one.
    6. Resolve. Resolving needs a resolution code and notes. The reader is emailed and asked to accept.
    7. Accept. If the reader accepts, the task closes, and the assignee is told.
    8. Reject. If the reader rejects, it goes back to the author. If they do neither, it stays resolved.

    Download the video (MP4)

    1. Open the task from the email or from My Assigned Tasks. Read the description: it is the reader's comment. E01 E07 E17
    2. Set the state to Work in progress. E05
    3. If you need more from the reader, set Awaiting information and write your question in Additional comments: it is mandatory in that state, and the reader is emailed. E12 E20
    4. Fix it:
      • Edit Article opens the article. Versioning is on, so change a published article through a new version: see Update a published article. E09 E23
      • Create Article starts a new article from the task, when the answer belongs in an article of its own. E10
    5. Set the state to Resolved, choose the Resolution Code and write the resolution notes. With Created article, fill Outcome with the new article. E11

    The reader is emailed "Your Feedback Task … has been resolved" and asked to accept. E20

    Resolution codes

    CodeUse when
    Updated articleyou corrected the article
    Created articleyou wrote a new one (set Outcome)
    Updated search configurationthe article was right but hard to find
    No actionthe feedback doesn't need a change
    Othersanything else; say what in the notes

    E05 E11

    Hand it on

    If someone else should do it, change Assigned to; the new assignee is emailed "Feedback Task … has been reassigned to you". E14 E18 A knowledge base's owner or manager can take any task on it with Assign to me. E15 E22

    After you resolve it

    Only the reader who raised the task sees Accept and Reject. E13

    They chooseThe taskYou get
    AcceptClosed"Your resolution to the Feedback Task … has been accepted" E13 E21
    Rejectback to Work in progress"… has been rejected": look at their comments and try again E13 E21

    If they do neither, the task stays Resolved: no job, rule or flow in the mirror closes it. E13 Q01

    Who sees what

    • You can open a task if it is assigned to you, you raised it, or you may contribute to the article's knowledge base. E16
    • Only the assignee, knowledge administrators and the knowledge base's owner and managers can change its state. E14 E15
    • Comments on the task (Additional comments) reach the reader; the assignee is emailed when someone adds one. E19 E20

    Tips

    • Answer the reader in Additional comments, in plain words: they see it, not your work notes.
    • Resolve with No action when the article is right, and say why: the reader can still reject.
    • Check My Flagged in the Knowledge Center now and then: an article stays flagged while it has open flags. E07

    Open questions OPEN QUESTION

    • Q02 Should a No, or a star rating at or below a threshold, ask the reader why and create a feedback task, as a flag does? Decision, for the process owner
    • Q19 With KM Advanced's assignment, a revised article's feedback goes to its last reviser, not its author; is that what the knowledge base owners expect? Decision, for Knowledge manager
    • Q05 Who owns the knowledge gaps reported from incidents, which have no assignee and send no email? Decision, for the process owner
    • Q37 Are the Feedback Management lists in the Knowledge Center's navigation as described, and what does a feedback task's form show? Check on screen, for the platform team
    • Q01 Nothing closes a Resolved feedback task the reader never accepts; should a scheduled job close such tasks after a set number of days? Decision, for the process owner
