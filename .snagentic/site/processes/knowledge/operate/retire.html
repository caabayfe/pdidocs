    5. Retire or replace an article

    For: Author, Knowledge managerWhere: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror 9f3658cfb43d)

    When to retire NEW

    Retire an article when what it says is no longer true and can't be fixed by an update: the product or service is gone, the procedure no longer exists, or another article now covers it. When it is only out of date, update it instead; retiring takes it away from readers. E18

    If another article now answers the same question, name it as the replacement when you retire: readers who follow an old link land on the new one. Replacements are switched on for pdi2. E06 E16

    Who can retire NEW

    You can retire the latest version of a published article if you are E04 E05:

    • its author or reviser, and you may contribute to its knowledge base; or
    • the knowledge base's owner or one of its managers, or an admin.

    Drafts and older versions show no Retire button. E04

    Retire an article NEW

    Transcript (6 steps)
    1. Start. Open the article in the Knowledge Center and choose retire. Only the latest published version can be retired, by its author or reviser, or by the knowledge base's owner or a manager.
    2. Replacement. The retire confirmation asks whether another article replaces this one. Choose yes and search for the replacement by number or short description, or choose no.
    3. Flow. Retire article starts the knowledge base's retire flow. With the instant flow, the article is retired at once.
    4. Retired. Every version of the article is retired, and it no longer appears in search or in knowledge lists.
    5. Approval. With the approval flow, it waits in pending retirement, and the owner and managers are asked to approve. If they reject it, the article goes back to the state it had.
    6. Readers. A reader who follows an old link lands on the replacement, with a message that the old article was retired. Without a replacement, readers can no longer open it.

    Download the video (MP4)

    1. Open the article in the Knowledge Center and choose Retire in the action bar. E01 Q39
    2. The Retire Confirmation dialog asks: "Do you want to replace the current article with another existing article?" E02
    3. Choose No to retire it without a replacement, or Yes and then Select Article: search by article number or short description. Only published articles, in knowledge bases of the same application, are offered. E02 E03
    4. Choose Retire Article. E02

    On the classic form, Retire opens the same Retire Confirmation question. E07 The plain Retire button without the question appears only when replacements are switched off, so you won't see it on pdi2. E26

    What happens next NEW

    The knowledge base's retire flow decides. E08 Q24 Q15

    Retire flowWhat happensWhat you see
    Knowledge - Instant Retireretired at once E09"This knowledge item has been retired" E14
    Knowledge - Approval RetirePending retirement until the approvers decide; approved, it is retired; rejected, it goes back to the state it had E10"This knowledge item is pending retirement" E14
    • The approvers are the knowledge base's owner and managers; ownership groups are off on pdi2. E11 E12 Each gets "KB… is awaiting approval." with an Approve/Reject link, the same email as for publishing. E13
    • With a replacement, the message reads "The knowledge article KB… is retired and replaced with article KB…". E08
    • Every version of the article is retired, not only the one you had open. E15

    What readers see NEW

    The articleA reader who opens it, or follows an old link
    Retired with a replacementis taken to the latest version of the replacement, with "The article KB… has been retired and replaced with the current article." E16
    Retired without a replacementcan't open it; only knowledge administrators can E17 Q27
    Retired, either wayno longer appears in search or in knowledge lists E18

    If the replacement is itself retired later with a replacement of its own, readers are taken along the chain to the newest one. E16

    Bring an article back NEW

    Knowledge administrators see Republish on the latest version of a retired article. It is published again, and its older versions become outdated. E19 Q27 Its replacement is cleared, so readers stop being redirected. E20

    Valid to and expiring articles NEW

    Every article has a Valid to date. E25 For a new article it is filled from the knowledge base's Article Validity (a number of days); a knowledge base without one gives 1 January 2100. E21

    • After its Valid to date an article drops out of readers' lists and search, even though it is still published. E18
    • The Knowledge Center lists articles due within 90 days under Articles › Expiring Articles, and retired ones under Articles › Retired. E22 E23
    • Nobody is warned by email: the monthly expiry warning is switched off on pdi2. Check the Expiring Articles list yourself. E24 Q09

    For an expiring article, either update it and move its Valid to date on, or retire it.

    Tips

    • Prefer a replacement whenever one exists: links in old incidents, emails and bookmarks keep working. E16
    • Before retiring, check the article isn't the replacement of other retired articles: they would stop redirecting anywhere useful. E16 E17
    • If an article only needs a correction, check out a new version rather than retiring it. See Update a published article.
