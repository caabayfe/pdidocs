    Knowledge management

    Write, publish, keep current and retire knowledge articles in the Knowledge Center: each knowledge base decides who reads, who writes and whether publishing needs approval, and feedback from readers comes back as tasks.

    Where: Service Operations Workspace, the classic incident formChecked against pdi2 on 2026-10-20 (mirror 9f3658cfb43d)

    Who this is for

    • Author — writes and updates articles in the knowledge bases they may contribute to (the knowledge role, or itil when writing from an incident)
    • Knowledge manager — the owner or a manager of a knowledge base (knowledge_manager); approves publishing and retiring where the knowledge base asks for it, and works the feedback on its articles
    • Quality reviewer — checks articles against the quality checklist (knowledge_coach, knowledge_domain_expert)
    • Knowledge administrator — creates and sets up knowledge bases, templates and who may read and contribute (knowledge_admin)

    How it works

    • How knowledge management works
    • The life of an article
    • Who is told what

    What you can do

    • 1. Write an article
      Create a draft in the Knowledge Center, in the right knowledge base and category, from a template where one applies.
    • 2. Publish an article
      Publish a draft, at once or after the knowledge base's approvers agree, now or on a set date.
    • 3. Update a published article
      Check out a new version, edit it and publish it; the old version becomes outdated.
    • 4. Work feedback and knowledge gaps
      Act on feedback tasks raised by flags, low ratings and reported gaps, and resolve them by fixing or writing an article.
    • 5. Retire or replace an article
      Withdraw an article that is no longer right, point readers to its replacement, and watch expiring articles.
    • 6. Check article quality
      Score an article against the quality checklist (AQI) and send the results to its author.
    • 7. Set up knowledge bases
      Request or create a knowledge base, name its owner and managers, choose who may read and contribute, and choose its publish and retire flows.

    Lifecycle

    1. Draftbeing written; only authors and managers see it
    2. Reviewwaiting for approval to publish
    3. Scheduled for publishapproved; it is published on its publish date
    4. Publishedreaders can find it
    5. Pending retirementwaiting for approval to retire
    6. Retiredwithdrawn; readers no longer find it
    7. Outdatedan older version, replaced by a newer published one
