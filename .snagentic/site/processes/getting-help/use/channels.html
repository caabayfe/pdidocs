    Ways to get help

    Last checked 2026-10-20

    You can reach IT in four ways: Employee Center, chat, email and phone. Whichever you use, you get the same thing back: a numbered ticket, an email confirming it, and the same place to follow it, My Requests in Employee Center.

    Transcript (6 steps)
    1. Employee Center. Most help starts in Employee Center. Search for what you need, or choose a topic.
    2. Chat. For a quick question, the chat button at the bottom right opens Now Support, the virtual agent.
    3. Email. Away from the portal? A new email becomes an incident, and a reply adds to the ticket it answers.
    4. Phone. When it's urgent and you can't work, call the service desk, from nine to five Pacific time.
    5. One ticket. Whichever way you choose, you get one ticket, with its number.
    6. Follow it. You get an email at each step, and you follow it in My Requests.

    There's no walk-up desk to visit: Get support lists only the phone. Teams, Slack and text messages aren't set up.

    Employee Center

    Employee Center is where most help starts and where all of it is followed. Search for what you need, or browse a topic, then report a problem or order something. See Using Employee Center for a tour, Report an incident and Find what you need.

    Chat with Now Support

    1. Press the round chat button at the bottom right of any Employee Center page.
    2. Now Support opens and greets you: "Hi, I'm your Virtual Agent. Let me know how I can help you today."
    3. Type your question in your own words, or press Show me everything to see what it can help with.

    The Virtual Agent answers your questions. Nobody from the service desk answers chats yet, so the chat can't put you through to a person. To report a problem or ask for something, use Employee Center or the phone.

    Email

    • A new email becomes an incident. Your subject becomes its title and your message its first comment, and the incident is yours. Write the subject as you would a title: "VPN disconnects every 5 minutes".
    • Forwarding works too. Forward an error message you received; the incident is yours.
    • Reply instead of writing again. Replying to an email about your incident or request adds your reply to it. A new email starts a new incident.
    • High importance means "I can't work". It sets the highest impact and urgency, so the incident is treated as the most urgent. Use it only when that is true.
    • Write from your work address. Email from an address IT doesn't know doesn't create an account for you.

    Phone

    Call +1-200-120-2020, 9am to 5pm PST. The service desk agent logs an incident while you talk, and you get the same "Incident … was created" email as from Employee Center. Keep the number it gives you, and follow it in My Requests.

    What happens next

    Every way leads to the same ticket. You follow it in My Requests, answer questions in its Activity, and get an email at each step: see Follow your incident and Your request's statuses and emails.

    Tips

    • One problem, one ticket. If you already reported it, reply to its emails or add to it in My Requests instead of reporting it again.
    • Not sure whether it's broken or something new? Search Employee Center: the results show both the forms to report problems and the items you can order.
